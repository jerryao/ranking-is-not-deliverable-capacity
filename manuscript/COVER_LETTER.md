# Cover Letter

**Manuscript:** Ranking Is Not Deliverable Capacity: Operational Calibration and Stress Testing in a Sichuan-Informed Demand-Response Benchmark

**Journal:** Frontiers in Energy Research

---

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit our revised manuscript for consideration by *Frontiers in Energy Research*. This paper introduces the concept of a **ranking--calibration gap** in demand-response capacity commitment: a resource ranking model can achieve high rank correlation with actual delivery ($\rho \approx 0.89$) yet fail to support reliable portfolio-level capacity commitments.

**Key findings.** In a Sichuan-informed simulation benchmark calibrated with empirical demand-response delivery ratios from PJM, MISO, and NYISO, we show that:

- An uncalibrated capacity heuristic achieves only 42.9% task success rate at moderate ($5\times$) requirement levels;
- Global linear calibration raises this to 87.3%;
- A split-conformal lower-bound estimator further improves it to 94.7%;
- Under standardised severe stress, even the routine-regime oracle achieves only 3.6%, demonstrating that stress transferability—not routine prediction accuracy—is the binding constraint.

These findings are robust across 30 independent benchmark realisations, 11 execution-realization-factor configurations, 14 stress scenarios, three distribution families, three persistence structures, and task-/user-/city-level holdout protocols.

**Scope statement.** This is a simulation-based methodological study. The 9 GW resource library and 26 VPPs provide real-world motivation; all user-level delivery values, task success rates, and over-commit rates are benchmark-generated. The execution realisation factor $\xi$ is calibrated to published aggregate delivery ratios from independent system operators, providing literature-based external anchoring but not direct validation against historical Sichuan event data. We clearly distinguish external calibration from external validation throughout the manuscript.

**Revision summary.** The reviewer's comments were instrumental in improving the manuscript. In particular, the reviewer's observation on base-field specification (Item 3) led us to identify and correct a response-rate double-discounting issue, add a literature-calibrated execution realisation factor, and upgrade from a single-seed deterministic evaluation to a 30-seed design with Wilson confidence intervals. The stress analysis was expanded from a single extreme scenario to a 14-configuration sweep. Two new calibration methods (isotonic regression and split-conformal lower bounds) were added in response to the reviewer's request for nonlinear and uncertainty-aware methods.

We believe this manuscript is well-suited for *Frontiers in Energy Research* because it bridges the gap between individual-level demand-response prediction and portfolio-level operational reliability—a practically important problem for load-management programmes in China and internationally.
